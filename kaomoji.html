What is Kaomoji? The Art of Japanese Text Emoticons

Kaomoji (顔文字), literally translated as "face characters," originated in Japan during the mid-1980s. Unlike Western ASCII smileys like :-) which must be tilted 90 degrees to understand, Japanese kaomoji are designed to be read straight-on. They use Japanese characters, katakana, math symbols, and diverse punctuation marks to represent expressive facial gestures, eyes, and hands.

Popular examples include the classic Shrug Kaomoji ¯\_(ツ)_/¯, the Table Flip (╯°□°)╯︵ ┻━┻, the Happy Sparkle (✿◠‿◠), and adorable Cat Faces (=^･ω･^=).

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I type Kaomoji on Windows? ▼

On Windows 10 & 11, press Win + . (period) or Win + ; to open the Windows Emoji picker, then click on the Kaomoji tab ;-) at the top. Or use FunEmojis.app for 1-click clipboard copy of 150+ curated styles.

Why do some Kaomoji show as boxes on my phone? ▼

If an older mobile device lacks full Japanese Unicode font support, certain specialized Japanese kana characters might display as hollow boxes (tofu). All kaomoji on FunEmojis.app are tested for compatibility with modern iOS, Android, and Windows systems.
